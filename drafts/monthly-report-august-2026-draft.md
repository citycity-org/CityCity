- Inflation eased to **2.8%** in June (down from 3.2%), signalling moderating cost pressure for households.

## 1. Inflation: Pressure Easing but Not Gone

Canada's Consumer Price Index rose **2.8% year-over-year** in June 2026, down from 3.2% in May — a month-over-month decline of 0.4 percentage points. Excluding gasoline, CPI held at a steadier **2.2%**, suggesting the headline improvement is partly energy-driven.

For renters and homebuyers, this shift matters in a specific, practical way: moderating inflation reduces the pace at which everyday costs erode disposable income. Workers in cities with already-tight rent-to-income ratios — Vancouver, Toronto — get some breathing room, though the fundamental affordability gap remains structural.

**What it means for Lakive users:**  
Cost-of-living pressure is easing at the margins. The RPI (Rent Pressure Index) values used in Lakive's calculations are based on housing costs, not general CPI — so this month's CPI improvement does not trigger a model update. It does, however, reinforce that the worst of the inflationary cycle (2022–2024) appears to have passed.

## 2. Monetary Policy: Bank of Canada Holds at 2.25%

On July 15, 2026, the Bank of Canada maintained its overnight policy rate at **2.25%**, in line with consensus expectations. The Bank cited improving economic conditions alongside residual uncertainty in global energy markets and trade policy.

Key forward guidance:
- Inflation expected to continue declining gradually over the coming months
- Economic growth on a modest recovery path
- **Next rate decision: September 2, 2026**

**What it means for homebuyers:**  
The 5-year fixed mortgage rate in Canada currently sits in the **4.69%–5.14%** range depending on lender and term. Variable-rate mortgages track closer to prime (BoC + ~2.2% = 4.45%). With the policy rate unchanged, monthly carrying costs for a new buyer are stable month-over-month.

Lakive's mortgage calculator uses a **5.5% rate assumption** — a modest premium above current best available rates, appropriate for planning purposes given that rates can rise between now and closing, and individual borrower rates vary by credit profile and insurer.

**Next rate decision on September 2** will be the next significant event to monitor. A cut there would lower variable mortgage costs by approximately $25–$40/month per $500,000 of principal.

Manufacturing continues to shed positions, a pattern consistent with softer US demand and ongoing automation pressures.

## 4. Housing Market: Recovery Continues, Prices Broadly Stable

Canadian housing market activity continued its measured recovery through Q2 2026:

- **Transaction volumes** are rising across major markets — buyer return following the 2023–2024 slowdown
- **Prices** remain broadly stable compared to the sharp increases of 2021–2022
- **Buyer confidence** is improving, supported by rate stability and easing inflation
- **New listings** have increased in most markets, maintaining some balance

This pattern is consistent across Vancouver, Toronto, Calgary, and Ottawa — though the pace of recovery differs materially by market.

**Vancouver** remains supply-constrained. Price stability does not imply affordability — the structural gap between incomes and home prices is unchanged. HPI Years for most occupations in Vancouver remain in the L4–L5 range.

**Calgary** continues to stand out as the most balanced major Canadian market. Rising transaction activity has not materially eroded the city's relative affordability advantage for skilled workers.

**What it means for Lakive's housing data:**  
Rental and purchase price data in Lakive (HPI Years and RPI) are updated on a defined calendar tied to CREA, CMHC, and Rentals.ca releases. The current H1 2026 data set remains valid. The next refresh will occur when Q2 2026 official figures are confirmed.

## 5. Summary: What This Means for Homebuyers and Renters

| Economic Signal | Direction | Impact on Buyers | Impact on Renters |
|----------------|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| CPI 2.8% ↓ | Positive | Lower cost-of-living erosion | Reduced everyday cost pressure |
| BoC rate 2.25% (held) | Neutral | Mortgage costs stable | No direct impact |
| Unemployment 6.5% ↓ | Positive | Better income security | Better job stability for lease qualification |
| Housing prices stable | Neutral | No window closing or opening | No new rent surge expected |

**Bottom line for users considering a move:** The macro environment in July 2026 is more settled than at any point since 2022. Inflation is declining, rates are stable, and the job market is improving modestly. This is not a moment of urgency in either direction — it's a reasonable planning environment.

The structural affordability differences between cities — Calgary vs. Vancouver, Ottawa vs. Toronto — remain the dominant factor in Lakive's analysis and are unchanged by this month's macro data.
