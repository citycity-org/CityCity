**By Lakive Research** | Published July 2026

## Executive Summary

LinkedIn recently published its *2026 Cities on the Rise* report, spotlighting 25 U.S. metros where job opportunities are expanding fastest. Canada was notably absent from the list.

This report fills that gap — with a sharper lens. Rather than measuring city-level job growth in aggregate, Lakive breaks opportunity down by occupation. Because a city booming in tech hiring means little if you're a nurse. And a city with high vacancy rates for trades workers matters a great deal if you happen to swing a hammer for a living.

We analyzed five major Canadian cities — Vancouver, Toronto, Calgary, Montréal, and Ottawa — across three dimensions: Employment Opportunity Index (EOI), housing affordability, and overall city-worker fit. The result is a clear picture of which cities are rising for which workers, and why that matters for anyone planning a career move in 2026.

**Key findings at a glance:**

- **Calgary** leads Canada for trades workers and healthcare professionals, combining strong hiring demand with the most affordable housing of any major city.
- **Toronto** offers the broadest employment opportunity — the only city where 15 of 20 tracked occupations register High EOI — but housing accessibility has reached a generational low, and the gap between opportunity and affordability is widening.
- **Ottawa** punches above its size for public sector, legal, and healthcare workers, with a far more balanced affordability profile than Toronto or Vancouver.
- **Vancouver** remains the premier destination for tech and senior healthcare professionals, but its housing crisis increasingly limits who can realistically build wealth here.
- **Montréal** is Canada's value city — genuinely affordable, culturally rich, and a rising force in AI, gaming, and aerospace, but employment demand across most occupations still trails the other four cities.

At Lakive, we believe the best career move is one that builds your life — not just your résumé. The data in this report points toward a Canada where opportunity is more geographically distributed than conventional wisdom suggests. The question is no longer "which city has the most jobs." It's "which city gives you the best chance of thriving — financially, professionally, and personally."

## Methodology

### Employment Opportunity Index (EOI)

EOI measures the relative ease of finding quality employment in a given city for a given occupation. It draws on data from Canada's Job Bank, provincial licensing bodies, and Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey, and is expressed in three tiers:

- **High** — Strong demand, active hiring, multiple employers competing for qualified candidates
- **Mid** — Steady demand with normal hiring cycles; competition is moderate
- **Low** — Oversupply of candidates, limited active postings, or structural barriers to employment

### City Fit Score

A composite score (0–100) that weighs employment opportunity, housing affordability, rent-to-income pressure, and city infrastructure. Higher is better.

### How the Score Is Built

```
Job Bank EOI data  ──┐
CMHC housing data  ──┤──► City Fit Score (0–100)
StatCan LFS data   ──┤       ↕
Rent / income ratio──┘   HPI Years · RPI
```

### Housing Pressure Indicators

- **HPI Years** — How many years of full occupational income it takes to purchase a median-priced 2-bedroom home (lower is better)
- **RPI (Rent Pressure Index)** — Percentage of monthly gross income consumed by median 2-bedroom rent (lower is better)

*Data reflects 2026 H1 conditions. All figures are in Canadian dollars unless otherwise noted.*

>

## Part 2: City Profiles

### 🔴 Calgary — Canada's Trades & Healthcare Capital

**City Fit Score: 79 (average across occupations)**  
**Province:** Alberta | **No provincial income tax** | **Median 2BR rent:** ~$1,950/mo

> **Best For:** Trades workers, healthcare professionals, and anyone who wants to own a home and build financial security without sacrificing career quality.

Calgary is Canada's most compelling story in 2026. For the second consecutive year, it leads national rankings for working professionals in trades and healthcare — not because it's glamorous, but because the numbers add up unlike anywhere else.

The city's No Provincial Income Tax advantage (Alberta levies GST only — no PST) effectively gives every worker a raise of $3,000–$8,000 annually compared to Ontario or BC equivalents. Add housing that remains attainable by major-city standards, and the math becomes difficult to argue with.

**Who Calgary is for:**

| Occupation | Fit Score | EOI | HPI Years |
|------------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Family Physician | 92 | High | 2.5 yrs |
| Electrician | 91 | High | 3.9 yrs |
| Registered Nurse | 86 | High | 4.5 yrs |
| Lawyer | 86 | Mid | 4.2 yrs |
| Civil Engineer | 82 | High | 6.0 yrs |
| Plumber | 80 | High | 7.1 yrs |
| Truck Driver | 82 | High | 5.5 yrs |
| Police Officer | 84 | High | 4.8 yrs |

**What's driving growth:** Energy sector stabilization has restored confidence in Alberta's economy, while Calgary's tech corridor — anchored by the downtown innovation district — is attracting new employers. Healthcare hiring is running at a multi-year high, driven by population growth and backlogs post-pandemic.

**The catch:** Calgary's tech EOI (software engineers, data analysts) registers Mid, not High — the ecosystem is growing but still significantly smaller than Toronto or Vancouver. Culture and transit infrastructure also trail the larger metros.

### 🔵 Ottawa — The Underrated Career City

**City Fit Score: 72 (average across occupations)**  
**Province:** Ontario | **HST 13%** | **Median 2BR rent:** ~$2,100/mo

> **Best For:** Public sector professionals, lawyers, nurses, and tech workers who want Toronto-level career access at a fraction of Toronto's housing cost.

Ottawa rarely makes headlines, but for certain professionals it's quietly the best-run major city in Canada for career building. Government employment anchors the city's labour market, creating stable, recession-resistant demand for lawyers, policy analysts, IT professionals, nurses, and teachers. It's also Canada's fourth-largest tech market by employment.

Compared to Toronto — same province, same taxes — Ottawa's housing costs are nearly 30% lower, its commute times are shorter, and its Quality of Life index (Numbeo 2026) ranks it first in Canada.

**Who Ottawa is for:**

| Occupation | Fit Score | EOI | HPI Years |
|------------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Family Physician | 88 | High | 3.0 yrs |
| Lawyer | 84 | High | 5.0 yrs |
| Registered Nurse | 82 | High | 6.5 yrs |
| Software Engineer | 80 | High | 6.2 yrs |
| Secondary Teacher | 80 | High | 7.0 yrs |
| Police Officer | 80 | High | 6.8 yrs |

**What's driving growth:** Federal government digital transformation programs have created sustained demand for software engineers and data professionals. Healthcare expansion at The Ottawa Hospital and Queensway Carleton continues to absorb nursing graduates. The University of Ottawa and Carleton create a steady talent pipeline that also attracts employers.

**The catch:** Ottawa's private sector is smaller than its government presence, which can limit entrepreneurial and startup opportunities. The city also has fewer cultural amenities and international connections than Toronto, Vancouver, or Montréal.

### 🟡 Toronto — The Opportunity Capital, At a Price Few Can Afford

**City Fit Score: 65 (average across occupations)**  
**Province:** Ontario | **HST 13%** | **Median 2BR rent:** ~$2,750/mo

> **Best For:** High earners — senior physicians, lawyers, software engineers, and finance professionals — for whom Toronto's depth of opportunity justifies its cost. For everyone else, the math increasingly points elsewhere.

Toronto is Canada's undisputed employment capital. Of the 20 occupations Lakive tracks, 15 register High EOI in Toronto — meaning active hiring, multiple employers competing, and real negotiating leverage for qualified candidates. No other Canadian city comes close to this breadth.

But housing accessibility has reached a generational low. At 9.2–13.8 years of median occupational income to purchase a median 2BR home depending on your field, Toronto demands an extraordinary long-term commitment before home ownership becomes realistic — and for mid-income workers, it may never become realistic at all. A registered nurse in Toronto would need 12 years of full gross income to buy a median home. An accountant, 13.8 years. An electrician, 12.5 years. These are not affordability headwinds — they are structural barriers.

For high earners — physicians, lawyers, software engineers — Toronto remains worth it. For the majority of working professionals, the calculus increasingly favours Ottawa (same province, substantially lower costs) or Calgary (no provincial tax, genuine home ownership within reach).

**Who Toronto is for:**

| Occupation | Fit Score | EOI | HPI Years |
|------------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Family Physician | 86 | High | 4.5 yrs |
| Software Engineer | 88 | High | 9.2 yrs |
| Lawyer | 82 | High | 7.6 yrs |
| Accountant | 72 | High | 13.8 yrs |
| Registered Nurse | 72 | High | 12.0 yrs |
| Electrician | 70 | High | 12.5 yrs |

**What's driving growth:** Toronto's financial sector continues to expand. AI investment is accelerating, with major announcements from Vector Institute and corporate AI labs. Healthcare hiring remains elevated. The city's immigrant talent pipeline is unmatched in Canada, which both creates and fills opportunities at the upper end of the skills market.

**The catch:** The gap between Toronto's employment breadth and its housing accessibility is the defining tension of the city in 2026. Workers outside the top income quartile face a structural disadvantage — strong career options, limited financial advancement. Commute times and cost of living erode net compensation advantages relative to other cities. Toronto offers unmatched opportunity and declining accessibility. Both things are true.

### 🟠 Vancouver — Tech and Healthcare, But at a Price

**City Fit Score: 64 (average across occupations)**  
**Province:** British Columbia | **GST 5% + PST 7%** | **Median 2BR rent:** ~$2,950/mo

> **Best For:** Tech professionals and senior healthcare workers with long-term earning potential who are willing to accept a delayed — or permanently deferred — path to home ownership in exchange for quality of life and career depth.

Vancouver is Canada's most livable city by international indices (EIU 2026 ranks it in the global top 10), and for tech professionals and senior healthcare workers, it remains the destination of choice. Its software engineering EOI is High, driven by an active ecosystem that includes major offices from Amazon, Microsoft, Electronic Arts, and a dense cluster of local studios and startups.

But Vancouver's housing crisis has reached a point where even high earners feel constrained. At 9.5 years of a software engineer's salary to purchase a median home — and 13+ years for most other occupations — the city is increasingly a place people come to earn, not to build lasting financial security.

**Who Vancouver is for:**

| Occupation | Fit Score | EOI | HPI Years |
|------------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Software Engineer | 84 | High | 9.5 yrs |
| Family Physician | 82 | High | 5.5 yrs |
| Lawyer | 78 | Mid | 8.1 yrs |
| Electrician | 72 | High | 13.0 yrs |
| Data Analyst | 72 | Mid | 11.5 yrs |
| Pharmacist | 74 | Mid | 10.5 yrs |

**What's driving growth:** BC's tech sector continues to expand, supported by immigration and the city's quality-of-life draw for international talent. Healthcare demand is structural and ongoing. Construction trades remain in High demand as development continues despite affordability headwinds.

**The catch:** Vancouver has the highest rent-to-income ratios of any city in this analysis. Workers in mid-income occupations — teachers, IT support, mechanics, social workers — face a particularly difficult trade-off: stable employment but limited financial advancement. The city's housing market scores remain among the most stressed in the world.

### 🟢 Montréal — Affordability, Culture, and a Quietly Rising Economy

**City Fit Score: 62 (average across occupations)**  
**Province:** Québec | **GST + QST ≈15%** | **Median 2BR rent:** ~$1,850/mo

> **Best For:** AI researchers, game developers, aerospace engineers, and workers in creative industries who want world-class sector depth at a fraction of the cost of Toronto or Vancouver.

Montréal is Canada's most affordable major city by a meaningful margin. A software engineer in Montréal faces 5.2 years of salary to buy a median home — versus 9.5 years in Vancouver or 9.2 years in Toronto. Rent pressure sits at 28%, the lowest of the five cities tracked.

The challenge is employment breadth. Across 20 occupations, Montréal registers High EOI in zero categories — the only city in this analysis with that distinction. Hiring exists, but it tends to be steadier and less competitive, which cuts both ways: less volatility, but also less negotiating power and fewer unsolicited opportunities.

Where Montréal punches well above its overall EOI ranking is in specific, globally significant industries. The Mila Institute makes Montréal a genuine global hub for AI research — drawing talent from MIT, Oxford, and DeepMind, and anchoring applied AI labs from Microsoft, Google, and Meta. The gaming sector (Ubisoft, EA, Warner Bros. Games, Eidos) employs over 15,000 people and generates $1B+ in annual revenue. And aerospace — Bombardier, Pratt & Whitney, CAE, Bell Textron — makes Montréal one of the world's top three aerospace clusters by employment, alongside Seattle and Toulouse. Workers in these fields find a city that combines world-class industry depth with housing costs that genuinely allow them to build a life.

**Who Montréal is for:**

| Occupation | Fit Score | EOI | HPI Years |
|------------|-----------|-----|-----------|
| Family Physician | 78 | Mid | 2.6 yrs |
| Software Engineer | 70 | Mid | 5.2 yrs |
| Electrician | 68 | Mid | 5.5 yrs |
| Teacher | 68 | Mid | 5.8 yrs |
| Nurse | 65 | Mid | 6.0 yrs |
| Lawyer | 72 | Mid | 4.5 yrs |

**What's driving growth:** The AI ecosystem is the clearest story — Montréal attracts research talent and applied AI investment disproportionate to its overall city size. Aerospace continues to generate stable, highly skilled employment. Gaming and creative tech remain a major economic pillar. Construction continues to drive trades demand.

**The catch:** French language proficiency is functionally required in most professional environments, which limits access for English-speaking newcomers. Quebec's combined tax burden (GST + QST ≈15%, plus high provincial income tax) offsets some of the housing affordability advantage for higher earners.

## Part 3: Which City Wins by Sector

### Healthcare
**#1 Calgary** — Highest EOI breadth, fastest path to home ownership for physicians and nurses  
**#2 Ottawa** — Strong government-backed healthcare infrastructure, solid affordability  
**#3 Toronto** — Highest volume of opportunities, but financially stressful for most roles

### Technology
**#1 Vancouver** — Deepest tech ecosystem, most international employers  
**#2 Toronto** — Rapidly growing, second-largest tech market in Canada  
**#3 Montréal** — Global AI and gaming hub; affordability makes the total package compelling for the right roles

### Skilled Trades
**#1 Calgary** — Dominant by every metric: EOI, HPI Years, rent pressure, fit score  
**#2 Ottawa** — Solid demand and better affordability than the coastal cities  
**#3 Vancouver** — High demand but the affordability trade-off is severe

### Legal & Finance
**#1 Toronto** — Canada's financial and legal capital; highest concentration of firms  
**#2 Ottawa** — Federal legal and regulatory work creates strong, stable demand  
**#3 Calgary** — Energy sector drives legal and finance hiring; tax advantage amplifies net income

### Education & Public Service
**#1 Ottawa** — Federal employment anchors demand; best balance of salary and cost of living  
**#2 Calgary** — Growing population creating new schools and services; tax advantage meaningful  
**#3 Montréal** — Affordable living partly offsets lower EOI and tax disadvantage

### Aerospace & Advanced Manufacturing
**#1 Montréal** — One of the world's top three aerospace clusters; Bombardier, Pratt & Whitney, CAE, Bell Textron  
**#2 Toronto** — GTA manufacturing and supply chain ecosystem  
**#3 Calgary** — Growing advanced manufacturing tied to energy sector diversification

## Conclusion

Canada's labour market in 2026 is not one story — it's five, and they diverge sharply once you factor in occupation and housing.

The dominant theme is a rebalancing away from the coastal giants. Calgary and Ottawa are quietly doing what Toronto and Vancouver cannot: offering meaningful employment opportunities at prices that allow workers to actually build wealth. That calculus is shifting career decisions for tens of thousands of Canadians each year.

For those committed to coastal cities, the math still works — but only at the upper end of the income spectrum. For everyone else, the rise of Calgary and Ottawa may be the most important career geography story of this decade.

The right city isn't the one with the most job postings. It's the one where your career and your life can grow at the same time.

**Finding the right job changes your career. Finding the right city changes your life.**
