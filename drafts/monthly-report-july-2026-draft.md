## 1. Executive Summary

July 2026 marks a meaningful inflection point for Canadian renters and prospective movers. National average asking rents have now fallen for 21 consecutive months year-over-year, reaching CAD $2,033 — a level last seen in early 2023. At the same time, the Bank of Canada held its policy rate steady at 2.25%, inflation continues to trend toward the 2% target, and the labour market added jobs for a third consecutive month in June.

The combined picture: affordability pressures are easing, but unevenly. Rents are falling nationally, yet select urban markets are showing early signs of stabilization. For anyone considering a relocation decision in the second half of 2026, the window of improved affordability may be narrowing.

## 2. National Rental Market Update

Source: Rentals.ca × Urbanation National Rent Report, released July 8, 2026

The national average asking rent stood at **CAD $2,033** in July 2026, representing a **4.3% year-over-year decline** — the 21st consecutive month of negative annual growth. This sustained softening reflects a combination of elevated purpose-built rental completions, reduced interprovincial migration, and lingering affordability fatigue among renters.

Despite the headline decline, the month-over-month trend tells a more nuanced story. Rents edged **slightly higher** on a monthly basis for the second consecutive month, suggesting the floor may be approaching in certain markets. **Toronto** is the most notable example: after significant year-over-year corrections, asking rents in the GTA appear to be stabilizing, driven by a modest uptick in absorption of newly completed units.

**What this means for Lakive users:**

Renters who have been waiting for further price drops may find that the best opportunity window — particularly in Toronto and surrounding areas — is now. Smaller cities continue to offer relatively better affordability, though the gap is narrowing as national supply catches up.

## 3. Housing Market Overview

Source: CREA (Canadian Real Estate Association) — July data pending, expected mid-August 2026

CREA's July 2026 national home price data has not yet been released at the time of this report. The most recent available data (June 2026) showed:

- National benchmark home price continued to stabilize after the correction period of 2023–2024
- Higher-density markets (Toronto, Vancouver) showed mild upward pressure as rate cuts attracted renewed buyer interest
- Calgary maintained elevated demand relative to supply, keeping prices firm compared to eastern markets
- Ottawa showed balanced conditions with modest price appreciation

**Outlook:** With the BoC rate at 2.25% and no further cuts expected until at least September 2, buyer confidence has returned selectively. First-time buyers in mid-tier cities are better positioned than at any point in the past three years.

## 4. Employment Snapshot

Source: Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey (LFS), June 2026 data
*Note: July 2026 LFS data releases August 7, 2026. This report uses the most current official figures.*

Canada's labour market continued its gradual recovery in June 2026:

- **~18,000 net jobs added**, sustaining positive momentum from spring
- **Unemployment rate: 6.5%** — down 0.3 percentage points from the prior month
- **Youth employment** improved for the second consecutive month, particularly in services and technology sectors
- **Manufacturing** remains the weakest segment, reflecting global trade headwinds and softening demand from key export markets

The June employment picture, while not robust, is directionally positive. The combination of falling rents, lower interest rates, and steady job creation creates a more favourable environment for workers considering relocation — particularly those in high-demand sectors (healthcare, skilled trades, tech) where Lakive's city scoring shows strong cross-city opportunity gaps.

## 5. Interest Rates & Inflation

**Bank of Canada — Policy Rate: 2.25%**
Announced: July 15, 2026 | Next decision: September 2, 2026

The Bank of Canada held its overnight rate at **2.25%** at its July meeting, in line with market expectations. The Bank signalled that economic growth is expected to improve gradually and that inflation is on track to return to approximately 2%.

This rate represents a significant easing cycle from the peak of 5.0% in 2023. The practical impact:

- A typical 25-year mortgage of $500,000 at a 5-year fixed rate has become materially more affordable compared to 2023 peaks
- The rate hold (rather than another cut) signals the Bank is watching for sustainable evidence of 2% inflation before moving further

**CPI Inflation: 2.8%** (June 2026, latest available)
Statistics Canada releases July 2026 CPI data on **August 17**. Lakive will update its Cost of Living and Economic Overview modules immediately upon release.

The 2.8% inflation rate — down from highs above 8% in 2022 — means real purchasing power is recovering for Canadian workers. In cities where rent has also fallen, the compound effect is a meaningful improvement in day-to-day affordability.

## 6. Lakive City Insights

Based on updated July 2026 data across Lakive's five core Canadian cities:

**Calgary** continues to rank highly for workers in trades and energy sectors, though housing affordability pressure remains above the national average. Rental stabilization here happened earlier (2025) and the market has remained firm.

**Ottawa** offers strong public sector and technology employment, with housing costs that remain more accessible than Toronto or Vancouver. June job data shows steady federal sector hiring.

**Toronto** is showing the most significant rental market shift: 21 months of YoY declines have materially improved the rent-to-income ratio for median earners. However, ownership affordability (Housing Years Index) remains stretched above 25 years for median-income households.

**Vancouver** maintains the highest home ownership barrier of any Lakive-covered city, though the rental correction has been significant. Workers in healthcare and technology continue to find Vancouver's career opportunity index among the strongest.

**Montréal** remains the most balanced of Lakive's core cities: below-average rent, improving employment, and a lower cost-of-living baseline than Toronto or Vancouver. For French-speaking professionals, it continues to lead on composite affordability.
